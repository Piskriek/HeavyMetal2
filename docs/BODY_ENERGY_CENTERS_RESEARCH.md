## 5. Pain, healing and the speed of release

### 5.1 What Ra says about pain

- Pain is a warning. Before the veil the mind could block it; pain then acted like a fire alarm that moves the body away from harm, and a burnt hand could be mentally numbed until healed (83.23, 83.24).
- The veil between conscious and unconscious made pain harder to control by thought, which created catalyst that was not usable before (83.26).
- In the body's daily use, pain signals alert the mind, and the mind then makes small unconscious movements that ease the distortion (94.3).
- Pain is the most common catalyst. Usually it is mental or emotional, sometimes physical or spiritual. The lessons are patience, tolerance and a light touch (34.6). In fourth density physical pain is slight and mental and spiritual pain is the catalyst (43.9†).
- Ra links a dull pain at the indigo centre to a limited viewpoint and stored energy that is not being used (99.5). Taking pain-dulling drugs can blunt the catalyst, and one entity's refusal of them kept it sharp (103.4, 103.5).
- A good attitude to pain helps evolution, but Ra does not make it a blanket rule (83.25).

### 5.2 What Ra says about fast and slow release

**Ra gives nothing on "released too fast = sharp pain" and nothing on itching.** I searched the archive for itch, tickle, tingling and sharp.

- No hit for "itch".
- "Tickle" appears once and means nothing: a tickle in the left or right ear has no different meaning (45.9).
- "Tingling" appears once: tingling and images in meditation show a blockage in the centre being stimulated, whose energy the electrical body then spreads out and weakens (50.12).
- "Sharp" appears only as sharpened catalyst (103.5).

Nearest Ra ideas, none of them the owner's claim:

- **Forcing the rise invites imbalance.** Trying to raise the kundalini meeting point without understanding the magnetic principles invites great imbalance (49.5). The proper way is slow: each experience observed, undergone, balanced, accepted and settled in order (49.6).
- **Relief without a new configuration returns.** Charged conditions removed by psychic surgery tend to come back; uncharged ones stay gone. What matters is the new configuration chosen in time/space (66.13).
- **Overdoing balancing.** A man who repeatedly drove himself up the centres from red, absorbed in the process and cut off from others, ended in inner pain (18.11).
- **A release can be a single event.** Sexual energy transfer is described as one release of built-up potential, shared upward from red (84.13).
- **Repression is the negative path's method** of handling distortion: repress, then bring it out in an organised way to enforce will (46.12). The positive path balances rather than represses (46.13).

### 5.3 Medical notes (mainstream, not Ra)

- **Pain is a protective warning, with two speeds.** Nociceptors signal a possible threat. Thin fast A-delta fibres give the early sharp "first pain" (about 5 to 30 m/s). Slow unmyelinated C fibres give the later, longer, duller "second pain" (about 0.4 to 2 m/s) [Wikipedia, Nociceptor].
- **Pain is not a gauge of damage.** Search-result summaries of IASP-based sources say pain severity does not track tissue damage, and that chronic pain can lose its warning role. I did not open these, so treat them as pointers.
- **Itch is not just weak pain.** Itch makes you move toward the spot (scratch); pain makes you move away (withdraw). Histamine itch is mainly carried by C fibres. A 2006 JCI review describes itch-specific spinal neurons in the spinothalamic tract distinct from pain pathways, while later reviews stress the overlap, and the exact relation is debated [Paus et al. 2006, JCI; Wikipedia, Itch].
- **Pain suppresses itch.** Painful stimuli, including scratching, noxious heat and capsaicin, can inhibit itch [Wikipedia, Itch]. A review summary in search results says scratch relief comes from pain and itch pathways being active together.
- **Referred pain.** Pain from an organ can be felt at a different spot because afferent fibres from different tissues converge on the same spinal neurons. This is the oldest explanation (convergence-projection), and the page lists criticisms; there is no consensus on mechanism [Wikipedia, Referred pain].

## 8. Gaps and honest limits

- **Itch:** nothing in Ra.
- **Fast vs slow release:** nothing in Ra beyond the loose analogies in 5.2.
- **Side:** nothing in Ra ties any ray or distortion type to a side. The only side facts are the head/shoulder field polarity (45.10, 49.4) and the hand gestures (97.16). Everything else is one person's case history.
- **Body parts per ray:** Ra names a zone for only red (root), yellow (solar plexus), green (heart), indigo (pineal, brow). No zone for orange, blue (beyond the throat cases) or violet.
- **Glands:** Ra's own words about glands are only a case-specific strain on spleen, adrenals, kidneys and liver after a bite (101.2†). Ra gives no ray-to-gland map.
- **Illness by ray:** only yellow (gut, 102.2), indigo (pain and tenderness, 9.23, 99.5), blue (throat cases) and red (anger-growth) have anything. Orange and green have none that is explicit.
- **Method:** the archive search is keyword-based; synonyms like "ache" or "tension" were not all searched. The pages were read through a summarising tool. Sessions not opened are marked †.
- **Secondary sources are weak where they cover chakra-gland maps:** the best table I found is a wellness site. Treat it as a convention, not a reference.
