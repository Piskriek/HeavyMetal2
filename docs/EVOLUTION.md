# How Goblin Racing evolves

Goblin Racing is not a finished game. It is a living one that the players steer.

## The planet and the canon
When you enter the planet you get an instance of the **latest canon**: the newest agreed version of Goblin Racing. The canon is a
manifest of presets (rules, items, tracks, looks, sounds, interface, island) at specific revisions.

## Spectate, change, suggest or branch
You can watch any race and change anything while you watch. Because the simulation is deterministic, a spectator re-runs the race
on their own device from the same inputs, so "what if" edits are free.

If a change affects how the race plays, the game asks:

- **Suggest it** for the next evolution, or
- **Keep it as a branch** you can build on and suggest later.

Branches can branch. Every preset remembers what it was forked from, so the whole family tree is visible and mergeable.
Cosmetic changes (colours, sounds, HUD) stay yours unless you share them.

## Tournaments
Week-long tournaments have sign-up slots. If you do not show up for your turn you are penalised, so while you wait you spectate,
place bets with in-game currency, trade presets and mingle. Winners earn rating and a place in history.

## Statues and the next island
The winners' avatars are built into giant statues on the island of the next evolution.

## Majority rules
The next canon is chosen by **what players actually use**: for every part of the game (rules, items, tracks, looks, sounds,
interface) the most-used preset becomes the new default. The rules of the evolution itself are presets too, so the community can
change how it evolves.

## Spectating, bodies and classes
Spectating is a preset too. You can watch from a chase camera, a free camera, a cinematic director that jumps to the action, as a
ghost, or in first person, where you are an embodied goblin walking around the island. Goblins can die, go to jail and are
subject to goblin laws; the dead wait to be resurrected and look like ghosts until then, though they can still do everything.

After the first week of racing a shaman arrives and resurrects the bodies where they lie. He does it with the game's own presets,
and any player can copy him to make a class of their own. The next week racing balls start to break and a mechanic class appears
to repair everything, and that class can be copied too. Every week adds a mechanic and a class that answers it, and the world grows.
