## 2. Isekai and LitRPG conventions

**F1. The conventions are stable.**
- Isekai: a person is transported or reincarnated and usually gains a "cheat" ability; modern knowledge is often an advantage; the body sometimes changes. The 2012 *Sword Art Online* anime helped start the modern boom [52].
- LitRPG: visible stats and in-game progression; the protagonist knows the world is game-like. "Progression fantasy" is the cousin without game links [53].
- *Dungeon Crawler Carl* makes "the System" a character: an AI runs the dungeon and a broadcast show, supplies information, sets difficulty, gives loot for achievements, and is host and antagonist. A tutorial guide and a talking companion sit beside it. The first eight books sold over 14 million copies by July 2026 [54].

**F2. Readers want rules that matter, not numbers alone.** Wanted: mechanics that shape stakes and choices, real consequences, consistent rules, some way to see where you stand. Disliked: "numbers go up" without depth, window-dressing systems, pacing that suits neither fast nor slow readers [4]. This is expert opinion and forum talk; I found no survey.

**F3. Licensed isekai games copy the story and miss the systems.**
- *Slime ISEKAI Chronicles*: combat praised; town-building a hands-off checklist; skill trees without real choices; one dungeon layout reused [3].
- *SAO Alicization Lycoris* (6/10 in one review): about 15 hours of exposition and tutorial first; co-op and customization late; flat characters [55].

## 3. Dropped into an alien world: loop, companion, natives

| Game | Loop and guide | Natives | Praised / complained |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Outer Wilds** [6] | 22-minute loop; knowledge is the only progress; a ship log | Hearthians; extinct Nomai whose writing you read | Distinct planets; some got stuck without direction |
| **No Man's Sky** [5][62] | Survey, build, travel | Traders, later alien bases | F4 |
| **Subnautica** [7][63] | Survive, craft, escape; PDA signals nudge | Wildlife, ruins | Signals read as nudges to some, a "never ending tutorial" to others |
| **Starbound** [8] | Procedural planets, ship, quests | Towns of intelligent species | Metacritic 81 |
| **Kenshi** [9] | Squad sandbox, no chosen one | Factions react to notable deaths | Depth praised; grindy, UI cumbersome, brutal start |
| **Bannerlord** [40] | Clans and kingdoms | Eight kingdoms | World busy but mainly to be raided; diplomacy shallow |
| **Spore** [10] | Five stages; shared creations | Tribes, empires | Creator praised (100 million items by 2009); early stages thin |
| **Rain World** [11] | Survive a cycle; guide fades | Predators fight each other; scavengers can ally | Ecosystem praised; punishing, opaque |

**F4. No Man's Sky: sameness was fixed by adding systems.** At launch (2016) a critic called it "procedural oatmeal"; multiplayer was missing; Metacritic 61 on PC. Free updates added bases (Foundation), multiplayer and revamped planet generation (NEXT, 2018), biomes and creatures (Abyss, Visions), flora, fauna, binary stars and weather (Origins, 2020), companions (2021) and water, weather and biomes (Worlds Part I, 2024). It won Best Ongoing Game in 2020 and 2025 [5][62].

**F5. Hand-built identity per world works.** Outer Wilds gives each world its own look, music and gravity, and reviews name the variety [6].

**F6. A guide nudges; opinion splits on how hard.** Subnautica's signals point at places and players advise treating them as hints [63]. Outer Wilds has no guide voice [6].

**F7. Companions.** Destiny recast the Ghost and re-recorded every base-game line, because scheduling a film actor was hard; its lore sat in cards readable only outside the game [24]. EDI starts as a ship terminal with limits, then gains a body and a crew seat; players praise her wit, critics faulted her body design [23].

**F8. Natives with their own goals earn praise; shallow diplomacy earns complaints** [9][11][40].

## 4. Emergent native societies from seeds

**F9. Generate history before play and expose it as text.** Dwarf Fortress runs a chosen number of years before play and lists events as sentences in Legends mode, with an XML export; the interface is the usual complaint [13]. Caves of Qud builds history around five generated sultans whose accounts read like oral tradition and may contradict one another; Metacritic 91, 2025 Hugo Award [12]. A GDC talk covers the method (not opened; section 11).

**F10. Smallville (Stanford, 2023).** 25 agents with a memory stream, reflection and planning. From one seeded idea, a Valentine's Day party, they spread invitations and arrived together. Removing observation, planning or reflection each lowered believability [2].

**F11. Project Sid (Altera, 31 Oct 2024).** The PIANO architecture ran 10 to 1,000+ agents in Minecraft. They took specialized roles, followed and changed collective rules, and passed on culture and religion; the authors call it preliminary [1].

**F12. 2025-2026 follow-ups are research, not games.** Agents built from two-hour interviews of 1,052 people reached 83% of participants' own consistency on survey questions [60]. AgentSociety ran over 10,000 agents with about 5 million interactions [61]. I found no shipped game built on a 1,000-agent society.

## 5. AI NPC characters, 2024-2026

**F13. On-device small models have shipped.**
- inZOI's Smart Zoi: a 0.5B-parameter model, RTX GPUs only; players watch thoughts and steer by text [25].
- PUBG Ally: a 2B model plus speech in and out, all local, fitting GPUs with 8 GB total VRAM. A behaviour tree runs reflexes at tick rate; the model handles intent and speech. Scope was cut to one map and one mode [26].
- *Whispers from the Star* (Steam, 14 Aug 2025): talk to a stranded astronaut by text, voice or video; 92% of 202 reviews positive; the heavy AI use divides players [29].

**F14. Complaints: latency, cost, trust, shallow characters.**
- An 18-person VR study with GPT-4 Turbo: about 7 s average cycle latency, growing with context; realism 6.67/10, emotion and personality only moderate [31].
- Natural conversation needs under about 800 ms; cloud round trips add 1-2 s; cost is per generated line; unconstrained characters invent facts. *Where Winds Meet* limits LLM chat to side NPCs, text plus a few flags such as affinity. What works: tight prompts, limited knowledge, short replies, fixed actions, hand-written plot [32].
- The Bannerlord modder found ChatGPT slow, filtered and costly, and judged a randomizer more reliable than LLM quest generation [33]. In a 28-player text mystery, GPT-4's variation let players create story nodes the author never wrote [30].

**F15. Skyrim mods show the memory layer.** SkyrimNet gives each NPC a private memory from its own point of view, retrieved by meaning with a local embedding model, trivial memories fading; NPCs react only to what they could see or hear. Models run through cloud APIs; the default needs no GPU [27]. Mantella chains speech-to-text, a model and text-to-speech [28].

**F16. Low-end hardware.** The GTX 950M is a 640-core Maxwell part with 2 GB GDDR5 or 4 GB DDR3 [37]. Chrome's built-in model needs 22 GB free disk and over 4 GB VRAM, or 16 GB RAM and 4 cores [35]. WebLLM needs WebGPU, with no speeds given [36]. A CPU-only test on an older i5 with 12 GB: a 0.6B model 34-36 tokens/s, a 1B model 18.6, a 2B model about 10; 15-30 is called responsive [34].

## 6. "Build the way out" as the long goal

| Game | Goal | Where it drags |
|---|---|---|
| **Subnautica** [7] | Escape rocket from wreck blueprints | No quest structure |
| **Factorio** [38] | Launch a rocket; play on | Space Age (2024) adds four planets, each a different resource puzzle |
| **Satisfactory** [39][64][65] | Space Elevator, five phases, each unlocking tiers | Tier 7-8 burnout; thin endgame |
| **Raft** [41] | Visit all story islands | Slow start; thin postgame |
| **Dyson Sphere** [42] | A Dyson sphere across planets | Tedious early setup |
| **Astroneer** [43] | Seven Triptychs from seven worlds open a Gateway | Planets with little to do |

**F17. What satisfies.** A named goal in phases that each unlock something [39]; a goal that forces a visit to every world [43][38]; a difficulty dial: Satisfactory sets a 0.25x-100x delivery multiplier at start and lets you move the elevator [39].

**F18. Where it drags.** Mid-game infrastructure that only scales numbers [64]; early tedium [42]; nothing to build toward after the finale [41][65].

## 7. Co-creation multiplayer sandboxes

**F19. What lasts: layered tools and a safe default space.**
- Roblox: Luau in Studio; over 1,600 moderators, yet removal compared to "whack-a-mole". Safety changes: chat age checks (global Jan 2026), account tiers under 9, 9-15, 16+ (Jun 2026), age verification to publish [44].
- Garry's Mod: Lua addons became genres (Trouble in Terrorist Town, Prop Hunt, DarkRP); a malicious addon author (2022) [46].
- Minecraft: Realms are invite-only; the Marketplace passed $500 million by 2022 [50]. Dreams: remixable creations; live support ended Sept 2023 [45].

**F20. What goes wrong.** Rec Room built a ladder (Maker Pen, Circuits, Studio), reversed an unpopular Rooms 2.0 (Aug 2025), ended creator earnings (May 2026) and shut down (June 2026). Under-13 accounts had no voice or text chat, yet in April 2026 its friend-finder was used to match usernames to phone numbers [47]. UEFN's ranking is said to reward low-effort copies [48]. Core's output is called "shabby recreations" of big games [49].

## 8. Planets as beings and group minds

### (a) The planet as a character

| Game | The planet as a being | How its state shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Final Fantasy VII** [21] | The Lifestream sustains all life | Mako reactors drain it; 500 years on, Midgar is green |
| **Alpha Centauri** [59] | A dormant hive organism that speaks in dreams; Ascent merges human minds with it | Fungal blooms and mindworms undo terraforming |
| **Horizon Zero Dawn** [19] | GAIA, an AI terraformer | A recording explains her; critics faulted exposition through audio and text files |
| **Eco** [17] | Players share one ecosystem, with a meteor to stop | Pollution raises sea levels; society votes laws |
| **Terra Nil** [18] | You restore it, then leave no trace | Wasteland to forest with deer and bears; one critic said late number-chasing undercuts the calm |
| **Planet Crafter** (2024) [56] | Terraformation Index of oxygen, heat, pressure | Ecology shifts as it rises; 2 million copies by June 2026 |
| **Populous** [20] | Followers' numbers raise mana | Terrain you shape |
| **Avatar: Frontiers of Pandora** (2023) [57] | Eywa, the planet's living spirit | Clan favour buys gear; mixed reviews |

Spore lets you terraform a planet in seconds with heat and humidity tools [58]; Outer Wilds gives each world its own sound and physics [6].

**F21. Planet state shows through four channels:** a headline index (Planet Crafter), visible change in the land (Midgar, Terra Nil), a voice (Alpha Centauri's dreams) and reactions (blooms, sea-level rise). Players liked consequence tied to their choices and disliked exposition delivered only through logs [19].

### (b) Group minds

**F22. Geth (Mass Effect).** A geth is hundreds of networked programs, each too weak alone for more than basic movement and sensing. Bodies are "platforms" programs upload into. Pooled programs reason better as numbers grow, and Shepard can enter the consensus. Critics rank the geth among the franchise's best species and the geth-quarian choice among the trilogy's significant decisions; the pull is the individual (Legion) inside the collective [14][22].

**F23. Stellaris.** A Hive Mind has no elections and a population with no free will; drones cut off from the Mind fall into a coma and die. It gets +2% monthly Unity and +25% pop growth. Machine Intelligence is a networked mind of mindless work units plus a few semi-autonomous agents [15].

**F24. StarCraft's Overmind.** One central mind with a chain of command: cerebrates relay its will to lesser organisms. When it died the swarm became unstable [16].

**F25. Membership and memory differ by design.** Geth: connection is membership, memory is shared, individuals persist. Stellaris: membership removes the pop's will, and disconnection kills. Overmind: a hierarchy with a single point of failure.

## 9. Legal art (brief)

"Isekai" is a genre label [52]. In the cases found, what got challenged was named characters, franchises and mechanics, not an art style: Nintendo takedowns on Garry's Mod [46], infringing content and a trademark dispute on UEFN [48], and Nintendo's patent suit over *Palworld*'s catching mechanic (Sept 2024, unresolved on 22 Oct 2026) after experts said general creature ideas are not covered by copyright [51]. Rule: natives come from the preset ladder and original designs, never from a franchise's look or catchphrases. This is not legal advice.

## 10. Design implications for FIDELITY (proposals for the owner)

**P1. Five worlds in the first system (F5, F17).** Outer Wilds, Space Age and Astroneer use four to seven distinct worlds [6][38][43]. Seed one star system: the arrival world plus four. Each seed picks from the preset ladder: terrain, material stack, flora, one hazard, one signature rule, a music stem, a native society. A seed validator rejects a system where two worlds share the same hazard and the same resource puzzle. Audition seeds in the Studio ("we will see what the seeds produce").

**P2. Avoid No Man's Sky sameness (F4, F5).** Vary worlds by rule, not only by number: one world's resource comes from farming, another from ruins, another from cold [38]. Layer weather and hazards on top [62]. Give each world one hand-placed anchor site with writing the player reads [6].

**P3. Natives are ego presets with bodies (F8-F11, F15).**
- Each native is a Plan 56 ego preset (memory shorthand, distortions, dreams) with a body (§19, §20).
- The world seed runs a short pre-play history: three to five founders, one schism, one lost thing. It writes starting memories and a readable "legends" page [13][12]. Biased, contradictory accounts, as in Qud, match the ladder's distortions [12].
- Keep simulated named egos to dozens per world (Smallville used 25 [2]); the rest are counts, with Kenshi-style world states reacting to notable deaths [9].
- Behaviour comes from the ladder's weights, so the simulation stays pure; Smallville's memory, reflection and planning map to the shorthand, dreaming and weights [2].

**P4. Language is a tiered, optional layer (F13-F16).**
- Default: authored and grammar-built lines; no model; the deterministic packages are untouched.
- Optional tier behind a Graphics-style setting with Auto (toggle rule): output limited to text plus fixed flags, side natives only, grounded in that native's memory, plot lines authored [32][26]. Show a text bubble first and speak after, since 800 ms is natural and 7 s breaks immersion [32][31]. Split reflex from talk as PUBG Ally does [26].
- On the min-spec laptop, plan a 0.5-1B CPU model for short barks only [34]; do not require WebGPU or Chrome's built-in model [35][36][37]. Test before promising any of it.
- Tell players a model is in use; the AI divide is real [29].

**P5. The drone is "the System" voice (F1-F3, F6, F7).**
- Three channels: (1) a status window of derived values (the Four Floats of the player's build, rungs unlocked, titles), not a new global metric; (2) a notice voice for events (skill gained, title earned, quest logged) in the isekai register [53][54]; (3) an optional signal-style hint channel plus an Outer Wilds-style log of what was learned [6][63].
- Make numbers matter: a skill is a preset rung that changes what can be built or survived, not a checklist [3][4].
- Give the drone its own ego preset that grows from tutorial voice to companion, the EDI arc [23]. Its distortion state may flavour notices (an erratic System entertains in *Dungeon Crawler Carl* [54]) but must never hide needed information.
- Keep the voice swappable and the lore in-game [24]. Put a native and a build in the first twenty minutes; long tutorial openings drew complaints [55]. The isekai "cheat" is the player's command of the preset library [52].

**P6. The Stargate is a multi-world goal (F17, F18).**
- Phases that each unlock something, like the Space Elevator [39]. Each needs a component from a different world, learned from its natives, as Astroneer's Triptychs and Space Age force visits [43][38]. Native attitude gates what they trade.
- Each phase is a new idea, not bigger numbers, to avoid the tier 7-8 burnout [64]. Offer a cost multiplier at start and a movable gate [39].
- An endgame: dialing out to the next system and Plan 42's random gates, so finishing is a start [65][41].

**P7. Planet presets whose density rises with their people (F21, owner).**
- Density is derived from the existing Four Floats and the Overwatch coherence of the natives, not a fifth global metric. It rises as inhabitants grow, as Populous ties followers to power [20] and Alpha Centauri's Planet reacts to what is built [59].
- Show it through the four channels in F21: a headline value in the drone window, land change, a voice at thresholds, and reactions [56][18][59]. Do not tell the planet's story only through logs [19].

**P8. Natives pool into a group preset (F22-F25, owner).**
- Reuse Plan 56 §7: above a coherence threshold, memories merge into one social memory; below it they separate.
- Keep individuals inside the pool, so the player can enter a "consensus" view as in Mass Effect 3 and still meet Legion-like voices [14]; Stellaris shows the cost of removing them [15].
- Membership by connection: a member cut off from the pool aches (the §20 body zones), echoing Stellaris's coma [15]. Build pools, not hierarchies, so one death does not collapse a group [16].

**P9. Sandbox multiplayer (F19, F20).**
- Worlds are invite-only by default, like Realms; public listing after review [50][44].
- Creations are share codes with a remix chain. The drone is the creation assistant; the Studio stays the professional tool, a Maker Pen-to-Studio ladder [47].
- Surface content through the weekly majority harvest (Plan 56 §14), not raw popularity, to avoid copy-rewarding rankings [48].
- Child safety: no free text or voice for unverified or young accounts, presets-only communication, verification before publishing, no phone-number matching [44][47].
- Keep creations exportable and independent of payouts or a live server; Rec Room and Dreams both lost service [47][45]. Test for malicious presets [46].

**P10. Legal (section 9).** Review natives and the System's voice for franchise echoes before release, and check mechanics against known patent disputes [51].
