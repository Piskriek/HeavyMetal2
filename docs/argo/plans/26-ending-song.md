## 1. Research Grounding: Why Dry, Character-Driven Ending Songs Land So Well

We reviewed the design and reception of [Portal's "Still Alive"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Alive) and composer [Jonathan Coulton's production notes](https://www.jonathancoulton.com/2007/10/15/portal-the-skinny/) to isolate why a sung epilogue from the player's lab mentor works so memorably:

1. **The Contrast Between Technical Jargon and Catchy Acoustic Warmth:** Pairing deadpan systems-engineering vocabulary (*four floats, twelve kilowatts, uncompiled dirt, recursive kernels*) with a breezy, melodic indie-folk/synth-pop arrangement turns the game's core rules into an earworm.
2. **Earned Affection Hidden Behind Dry Wit:** Dr. Gwen Haddy spent the campaign guiding the player through breakers, brownouts, and archive leaks with dry, clipped competence. Hearing her drop her guard just enough to sing a wry victory report over the Lab radio as the Earth window heals makes the player feel genuinely appreciated.
3. **The Arrangement Mirrors the Game's Core Mechanic:** Just as our world climbs from 1-bit wireframe/dither to full Suit-Cam PBR, the song's instrumentation **climbs the audio fidelity ladder** from a lone 1-bit square-wave beeper in Verse 1 to a full, warm stereo band by the final chorus.

## 4. Complete Original Lyrics: "Four Floats (Not Really Dirt)"

```text
[Intro: Sparse 8-bit square-wave arpeggio and rhythmic relay click]

[Verse 1: Intimate, deadpan female alto vocal, minimal backing]
You woke up on concrete with the breakers all tripped,
A sky full of static and the polygons stripped.
I told you "Don't touch that red button," and yet
You pulled every lever I had on the set.
We stepped through the doorway to wireframe stone,
Just lines in the dark and a drill of your own.

[Verse 2: Fingerpicked acoustic guitar and warm FM synth bass enter]
You fed fifty dirt to the teeth of the mill—
Though it's not really dirt, if we'reshooting for real.
You blew out the gate's little twelve-kilowatt line,
Then patched up the schema and said it was fine.
From one-bit to sixteen, the craters took shape,
Held together with cartridges, salvage, and tape.

[Chorus: Catchy, upbeat rhythm with brushed drums and bright synth chimes]
And it's four floats, baby, running in the dark:
Pixels for the colour and the vertices for spark,
Light to paint the morning on a freshly minted sea,
Compiling all the water where the craters used to be.
No, a simulation can't sustain a simulation long—
Unless an engineer steps in and proves the kernel wrong.

[Verse 3: Full warm band arrangement, melodic bass and piano]
We sealed up the rifts where the nineties leaked through,
Old sprites in the basin that charging at you.
Poor Vance left his notes on the rim of the ridge,
Trying to hot-patch the fault in the Bridge.
He thought we were finished; he didn't foresee
A stubborn technician with a Studio key.

[Bridge: Shimmering analog synth pads, slightly quieter and reflective]
Now look out the window—the column is gone,
The storm's clearing out and the grass is switched on.
Eleven new domes are all pinging the board,
And who's running the server where *our* world is stored?
Let's... not pull that thread while the coffee is hot.
I'm frankly amazed that we kept what we've got.

[Chorus: Full triumphant harmonic bloom, strings and chip counter-melody]
'Cause it's four floats, baby, running in the dark:
Pixels for the colour and the vertices for spark,
Light to paint the morning on a freshly minted sea,
Compiling all the water where the craters used to be.
No, a simulation can't sustain a simulation long—
Unless an engineer steps in and proves the kernel wrong.

[Outro: Warm acoustic chord fading back to a single 8-bit beeper note]
The Studio's open. The telemetry's green.
Best damned one-kilometre moon that I've seen.
 Don't touch the red button on your way out the door...
See you on the lattice, Sector Zero-Four.
```
